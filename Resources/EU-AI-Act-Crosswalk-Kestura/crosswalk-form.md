**Contributor Notes:**

This crosswalk is a **draft first release** intended to help privacy programs identify how obligations of the EU Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) reinforce, extend, or overlap with the outcomes in the NIST Privacy Framework v1.0 Core, particularly for organisations that develop or deploy AI systems that process personal data.

**Scope and framing**

- The mapping is **bidirectional**: Tab 1 maps each of the 100 Privacy Framework Subcategories to relevant AI Act provisions; Tab 2 groups the AI Act provisions and lists the Subcategories they support.
- Provisions are cited by Article number (and Annex where applicable), consistent with the numbering of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as adopted.
- Where the AI Act does not directly address a Privacy Framework outcome (for example, individual data-subject rights of access, rectification, or portability), we note the interplay with the GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) rather than force a mapping.
- The crosswalk assumes the reader is applying the AI Act to **high-risk AI systems** unless a provision applies more broadly (Arts. 4, 5, 50, 85, 86, 95, 99–101). Obligations that apply only to providers of general-purpose AI models (Arts. 51–55) are referenced where relevant but are not the primary focus.

**Confidence key (used in the Notes column of Tab 1)**

- `[H]` — High confidence. The AI Act text directly aligns with the Privacy Framework outcome.
- `[M]` — Medium confidence. Same intent with partial overlap or a scope difference.
- `[L]` — Low confidence. Related theme; contributor judgement.

**Known limitations to flag for community review**

1. Data-subject-facing outcomes (CT.DM-P1–P4) rely partly on GDPR interplay because the AI Act itself does not grant access, rectification, deletion, or portability rights. Community input on how contributors are treating this in practice is welcome.
2. Robustness/cybersecurity obligations under Art. 15 are broad; we have mapped Art. 15 to a wide set of PR.* outcomes rather than attempting to sub-divide it. Feedback on tighter attribution (e.g. to specific Art. 15 paragraphs) is welcome.
3. Timing: obligations under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 phase in through 2025–2027. Users applying this crosswalk before their in-scope date should treat the mapping as a *readiness* view.

**Maintenance:** Kestura UG will maintain the file, incorporate community feedback via issues/PRs on this repository, and refresh mappings when NIST publishes Privacy Framework v1.1 or when the AI Act text is amended by delegated or implementing acts.
